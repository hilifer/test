**Short answer:** both tiers mean the fixture is on the DesignLights Consortium (DLC) Qualified Products List. Premium sets higher requirements, mainly for efficacy and lighting quality. Most North American utility rebates require at least a DLC listing, and some pay more for Premium.

## What DLC is

The DesignLights Consortium is a non-profit that publishes a list of commercial LED products that meet its performance requirements. Utilities use the list to decide which products qualify for rebates. It is separate from safety certification: a fixture still needs **UL or ETL** for electrical safety.

## Standard vs. Premium

- **DLC Standard:** meets the baseline requirements for efficacy, lumen maintenance and other quality criteria for its product category.
- **DLC Premium:** meets stricter thresholds, mainly higher efficacy (lumens per watt), so it uses less energy for the same light.

## Why it matters when you buy

1. **Rebates:** if your customer is counting on a utility rebate, the exact model must be listed before installation.
2. **Spec sheets:** many engineered projects specify "DLC Premium" by default.
3. **Model numbers:** the listing covers specific model numbers. Make sure the quotation and the listing match.
