**Short answer:** match lumens to mounting height first, then choose the optic for the space. As a starting point for general warehouse work, use 100–150 W up to about 20 ft, 150–240 W from 20 to 30 ft, and 240 W or more from 30 to 45 ft. Then confirm with a lighting layout.

## Start from mounting height

The higher the fixture, the more lumens you need to reach the same light level on the floor, and the narrower the beam can be. These ranges assume about 150 lm/W fixtures and typical warehouse light levels:

| Mounting height | Typical wattage | Approx. lumens | Common optic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15–20 ft (4.5–6 m) | 100–150 W | 15,000–24,000 lm | 120° |
| 20–30 ft (6–9 m) | 150–240 W | 22,000–38,000 lm | 90° |
| 30–45 ft (9–14 m) | 240 W and up | 36,000 lm + | 60° or aisle optic |

Tasks needing more light, such as inspection or assembly, move you up a row or call for tighter spacing.

## UFO or linear?

- **UFO high bays** such as the [UFO High Bay U2](/products/ufo-high-bay-u2/) are compact, hang from a single hook and work well over open floors. Wattage-selectable models let one SKU cover several ceiling heights.
- **Linear high bays** such as the [Linear High Bay L4](/products/linear-high-bay-l4/) are the better choice between tall racking, where an aisle optic keeps light on the shelf faces instead of the rack tops.

## Controls pay back quickly

Motion sensors and 0–10 V dimming cut energy use in areas that are empty much of the day. Many utility rebate programs in North America also require DLC-listed fixtures, so check the listing before you finalise a model.
